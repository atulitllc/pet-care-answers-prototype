Why does my cat knead?

By the Paws Answered editorial team · Published: Oct 13, 2026 ·🩺 Vet review pendingLast updated: Oct 13, 2026Reviewed: pending
⚠️ Not veterinary advice. If your pet is sick or hurt, contact a vet.
Short answer

Kneading is normal. Kittens knead their mother to help milk flow, and many adult cats keep doing it when they feel relaxed, to make a comfy spot, or to mark you with scent from their paws.

🚩 When to see a vet now

  • Sudden behavior changes along with pain, limping, or hiding
  • Not eating or using the litter box

In an emergency, call your vet or nearest emergency animal clinic.

🚨 Emergency: go to a vet now

  • Trouble breathing
  • Collapse
  • Seizures
  • Bloated belly or retching with nothing coming up
  • Suspected poisoning (chocolate, xylitol, lilies for cats, grapes)
  • Heavy bleeding
  • Male cat straining to pee
  • Pale gums

ASPCA Animal Poison Control: (888) 426-4435 (a fee may apply)

What the sources say

Kittens knead their mother while nursing, which helps bring milk down. The comforting feeling seems to carry into adulthood. [1]

Cats also knead to make a resting spot more comfortable, to show affection, and to mark territory, since they have scent glands in their paws. [1]

Kneading can also be a stretch. [2]

A cat kneading and purring alone in an unusual place, such as under a bed, may be self soothing because it feels unwell. Talk to your vet if kneading suddenly increases or comes with hiding. [1]

FAQ

Is kneading a sign my cat likes me?

Often, yes. VCA notes cats usually do not knead on people or animals they do not like.

Should I stop it?

It is normal behavior. VCA suggests redirecting a pet to chase a toy if kneading gets disruptive.
