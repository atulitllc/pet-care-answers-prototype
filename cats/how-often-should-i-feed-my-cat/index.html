How often should I feed my cat?

By the Paws Answered editorial team · Published: Oct 13, 2026 ·Last updated: Oct 13, 2026Written from the cited sources. Not reviewed by a veterinarian.
⚠️ Not veterinary advice. If your pet is sick or hurt, contact a vet.
Short answer

That depends on age. Cornell says kittens generally do best eating three times a day until about six months, then twice a day until their first birthday. After that, one or two meals a day works for most cats. Always leave fresh water out, and ask your vet how much your cat should eat.

What the sources say

Growing kittens need a lot of food for their size, so smaller, more frequent meals help: around three a day to six months, then two a day to one year. [1]

Grown cats usually do fine on one or two meals daily, and older cats can stay on that schedule unless your vet suggests a change. [1]

Leaving dry food out suits some cats, but others eat too much. Swap out any leftover food daily. [1]

Feed at times that suit your day, and stick to them. [1]

Keep water fresh and available, and treat treats as a small extra, about a tenth of the diet or less. [2]

🚩 When to see a vet

  • Your cat refuses food or eats much less
  • Sudden weight loss or weight gain
  • Trouble chewing or dropping food

In an emergency, call your vet or nearest emergency animal clinic.

🚨 Poison emergency: call now

  • Trouble breathing
  • Collapse
  • Seizures
  • Bloated belly or retching with nothing coming up
  • Suspected poisoning (chocolate, xylitol, lilies for cats, grapes)
  • Heavy bleeding
  • Male cat straining to pee
  • Pale gums

FAQ

Should I leave dry food out all day?

It can work for some cats, but not for those that overeat, according to Cornell. Refresh it daily.

Does my senior cat need a different schedule?

Usually not. Cornell says older cats can keep their routine unless your vet recommends otherwise.
