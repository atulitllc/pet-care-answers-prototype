Why is my cat throwing up?

By the Paws Answered editorial team · Published: Oct 13, 2026 ·🩺 Vet review pendingLast updated: Oct 13, 2026Reviewed: pending
⚠️ Not veterinary advice. If your pet is sick or hurt, contact a vet.
Short answer

Cats sometimes vomit from hairballs, eating too fast, or a sudden food change. An occasional episode in a cat that otherwise acts normal is often not an emergency, but repeated vomiting needs a vet.

🚩 When to see a vet now

  • Vomiting repeatedly in one day, or for more than a day
  • Blood in vomit, or vomit that looks like coffee grounds
  • Lethargy, hiding, or refusing food
  • Signs of pain, a swollen belly, or straining to pee
  • Possible swallowed string, toy, plant, or medicine

In an emergency, call your vet or nearest emergency animal clinic.

🚨 Emergency: go to a vet now

  • Trouble breathing
  • Collapse
  • Seizures
  • Bloated belly or retching with nothing coming up
  • Suspected poisoning (chocolate, xylitol, lilies for cats, grapes)
  • Heavy bleeding
  • Male cat straining to pee
  • Pale gums

ASPCA Animal Poison Control: (888) 426-4435 (a fee may apply)

What is going on

Hairballs are common, especially in long haired cats, and regular brushing may help.

Eating too quickly can bring food back up. A slow feeder or smaller meals may help.

Switching food suddenly can upset the stomach. Changes are usually made gradually.

Many illnesses can also cause vomiting, which is why a vet should check a cat that vomits often.

FAQ

Is it normal for a cat to throw up hairballs?

An occasional hairball can happen. Frequent hairballs or retching without producing one is worth asking your vet about.

Should I stop feeding my cat after vomiting?

Ask your vet before withholding food. Cats should not go long without eating.
