Why is my cat so itchy?

By the Paws Answered editorial team · Published: Oct 13, 2026 ·Last updated: Oct 13, 2026Written from the cited sources. Not reviewed by a veterinarian.
⚠️ Not veterinary advice. If your pet is sick or hurt, contact a vet.
Short answer

Itching is the most common sign of allergies in cats. Flea allergy is the most common, and one bite can cause intense itching, hair loss and scabs, often near the tail. Food and environmental allergies, mites and ringworm can look similar, so have your vet find the cause rather than guessing.

What the sources say

Itchy skin, in one spot or all over the body, is the most common allergy symptom in cats. [1]

Flea saliva is the most common insect allergen. A cat with flea allergy can react to a single bite with severe itching, scratching, hair loss and wounds, especially at the base of the tail. [1]

Cats with environmental allergies to pollen, molds or dust mites may overgroom, lose fur on the belly, or scratch at the head and neck. [1]

Food allergy can cause itching and stomach upset. Vets diagnose it with a strict elimination diet trial of about 8 to 12 weeks. [1]

Flea infestations, skin mites, ringworm, autoimmune disease and some cancers can look like allergies, so do not try to diagnose your cat at home. [1]

Strict flea control is the most important step for flea allergy. Ask your vet which flea product is right for your cat. [1]

🚩 When to see a vet

  • Scratching or overgrooming that causes hair loss, scabs or sores
  • Itching that keeps coming back
  • Itching with vomiting, diarrhea, sneezing or coughing

In an emergency, call your vet or nearest emergency animal clinic.

🚨 Emergency: go to a vet now

  • Trouble breathing
  • Collapse
  • Seizures
  • Bloated belly or retching with nothing coming up
  • Suspected poisoning (chocolate, xylitol, lilies for cats, grapes)
  • Heavy bleeding
  • Male cat straining to pee
  • Pale gums

FAQ

My indoor cat has no fleas I can see. Could it still be fleas?

It is possible. VCA says one flea bite can trigger intense itching in an allergic cat, so ask your vet about flea control.

How long does a food trial take?

VCA says an elimination diet trial usually lasts about 8 to 12 weeks.
