Can dogs eat chocolate?

By the Paws Answered editorial team · Published: Oct 13, 2026 ·Last updated: Oct 13, 2026Written from the cited sources. Not reviewed by a veterinarian.
⚠️ Not veterinary advice. If your pet is sick or hurt, contact a vet.

🚩 When to see a vet

  • Your dog ate any chocolate, cocoa powder or chocolate baked goods
  • Vomiting, diarrhea, panting or restlessness
  • Tremors, seizures or a racing heart

In an emergency, call your vet or nearest emergency animal clinic.

🚨 Poison emergency: call now

  • Trouble breathing
  • Collapse
  • Seizures
  • Bloated belly or retching with nothing coming up
  • Suspected poisoning (chocolate, xylitol, lilies for cats, grapes)
  • Heavy bleeding
  • Male cat straining to pee
  • Pale gums

ASPCA Animal Poison Control: (888) 426-4435 (a fee may apply)

Pet Poison Helpline: (855) 764-7661 (a fee applies)

Short answer

No. Chocolate contains theobromine and caffeine, which dogs are more sensitive to than people. It can cause vomiting, diarrhea, a racing heart, tremors and seizures. Darker and more bitter chocolate is more dangerous. If your dog eats chocolate, call your vet or a pet poison hotline right away.

What the sources say

Chocolate contains theobromine and caffeine. Dogs process these chemicals differently than people, which makes them more sensitive to the effects. [1]

The darker and more bitter the chocolate, the more dangerous it is. Cocoa beans, cocoa powder and baking chocolate have the most theobromine. White chocolate has the least. [1]

Common signs include vomiting, diarrhea, increased thirst, panting or restlessness, peeing more and a racing heart. Severe cases can cause abnormal heart rhythm, tremors and seizures. [1]

Signs can take several hours to show up and can last for several days. [1]

Even when the amount is not toxic, the fat and sugar in chocolate can still cause vomiting, diarrhea or pancreatitis. [1]

Chocolate candy and baked goods can also hide other poisons, such as macadamia nuts, raisins or xylitol. [3]

ASPCA Animal Poison Control lists chocolate, coffee and caffeine among foods to keep away from pets. [2]

FAQ

What if it was only milk chocolate or white chocolate?

Call anyway. Risk depends on the type and amount eaten and your dog's size. Your vet or a poison hotline can tell you if the amount is a problem.

Are dog treats made with carob safe?

VCA notes many dog treats use carob instead of chocolate. Most vets still advise not giving dogs chocolate in any form.
