Is xylitol toxic to dogs?

By the Paws Answered editorial team · Published: Oct 13, 2026 ·Last updated: Oct 13, 2026Written from the cited sources. Not reviewed by a veterinarian.
⚠️ Not veterinary advice. If your pet is sick or hurt, contact a vet.

🚩 When to see a vet

  • Your dog ate gum, mints, candy or any product that may contain xylitol
  • Vomiting, weakness or trouble walking
  • Tremors, seizures or collapse

In an emergency, call your vet or nearest emergency animal clinic.

🚨 Poison emergency: call now

  • Trouble breathing
  • Collapse
  • Seizures
  • Bloated belly or retching with nothing coming up
  • Suspected poisoning (chocolate, xylitol, lilies for cats, grapes)
  • Heavy bleeding
  • Male cat straining to pee
  • Pale gums

ASPCA Animal Poison Control: (888) 426-4435 (a fee may apply)

Pet Poison Helpline: (855) 764-7661 (a fee applies)

Short answer

Yes. Xylitol, a sugar substitute in sugar free gum, mints, candy, baked goods, some peanut butters and toothpaste, is extremely toxic to dogs. Even small amounts can cause a dangerous drop in blood sugar, seizures or liver failure. If your dog eats anything with xylitol, call your vet or a pet poison hotline now.

What the sources say

Xylitol is a sugar alcohol used as a sugar substitute. It may also be listed as wood sugar, birch sugar or birch bark extract. [1]

It shows up in sugar free gum, candy, breath mints, baked goods, peanut butter, pudding snacks, chewable or gummy vitamins, mouthwash and toothpaste. [1]

In dogs, xylitol causes a fast release of insulin. Blood sugar can drop as soon as 30 minutes to two hours after eating it. Larger amounts can cause liver failure within 24 to 48 hours. [1]

Sugar free gum is the most common source reported to Pet Poison Helpline. The amount of xylitol varies widely between products, and even between flavors of one gum brand. [2]

Signs of low blood sugar include vomiting, weakness, trouble walking or standing, tremors, seizures and coma. [1]

Do not make your dog vomit or give anything by mouth unless your vet tells you to. [1]

Only use toothpaste made for dogs. Never use human toothpaste on your dog. [1]

FAQ

How fast does xylitol work?

Fast. VCA says blood sugar can drop within 30 minutes to two hours, so call your vet as soon as you know.

Should I make my dog throw up?

No, not unless your vet tells you to. VCA notes some dogs may already have low blood sugar, and vomiting can make things worse.
