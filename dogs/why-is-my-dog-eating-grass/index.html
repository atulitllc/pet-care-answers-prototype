Why is my dog eating grass?

By the Paws Answered editorial team · Published: Oct 13, 2026 ·🩺 Vet review pendingLast updated: Oct 13, 2026Reviewed: pending
⚠️ Not veterinary advice. If your pet is sick or hurt, contact a vet.
Short answer

It is very common, and usually not a sign of illness. VCA notes most grass eating dogs are not sick beforehand and do not vomit afterward. See a vet if your dog also shows stomach discomfort.

🚩 When to see a vet now

  • Repeated vomiting or diarrhea
  • Not eating, lethargy, or signs of pain
  • Possible exposure to lawn chemicals or toxic plants

In an emergency, call your vet or nearest emergency animal clinic.

🚨 Emergency: go to a vet now

  • Trouble breathing
  • Collapse
  • Seizures
  • Bloated belly or retching with nothing coming up
  • Suspected poisoning (chocolate, xylitol, lilies for cats, grapes)
  • Heavy bleeding
  • Male cat straining to pee
  • Pale gums

ASPCA Animal Poison Control: (888) 426-4435 (a fee may apply)

What the sources say

Vets hear this question all the time, because many dogs eat grass. Eating non food items is called pica. [1]

VCA cites studies showing less than 25% of dogs vomit after eating grass, and only about 10% show signs of illness before eating it. [1]

Grass is a source of fiber, which may help digestion. Boredom or wanting attention can also play a part. [1]

If your dog shows stomach discomfort, a medical problem such as gastric reflux, inflammatory bowel disease or pancreatitis is possible, so see your vet. [1]

FAQ

Do dogs eat grass because they are sick?

Usually not. VCA notes only about 10% of dogs show signs of illness before eating grass.

When should I worry?

If your dog seems to have an upset stomach, vomits often, or eats grass frantically, see your vet.
